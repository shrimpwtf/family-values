# Delight: selective emphasis

## The principle

Simplicity and fluidity are the foundation. Delight builds the **emotional connection** between product and user. It is more than fun interactions: it is moments that resonate personally and make software feel more human and responsive. Building a great product means respecting not just what someone does, but how they *feel* while doing it.

Delight is subjective and styles vary. What matters is **the deliberate effort to make something special**.

## Prerequisite: even polish everywhere

You can only add delight anywhere if polish is consistent everywhere. Users notice when parts of an app are less refined, and that drags down the whole experience. Many products neglect their less-used features, which is "like going to a fancy restaurant but finding it has a dirty bathroom."

Every part of the app, however often it is used, gets the same holistic design attention. Every interaction is a potential moment of delight.

## The Delight-Impact Curve

**The potential for delight increases as a feature's frequency of use decreases.**

- **Rare features:** delight adds a lot. These moments leave the most lasting impression, through surprise and novelty.
- **Frequent features:** the value of extra delight shrinks with repetition. The specialness of a moment drops with each encounter, like each additional piece of your favourite candy. Frequent features still need to be inherently pleasant, but in an efficient and unobtrusive way.

(This echoes Rauno Freiberg's essay on frequency and novelty in interaction design.)

## Mastering selective emphasis

Mastering delight means knowing **where, when, and how** to apply magical moments across a product. Delight is not removed from common features. It is applied at different **intensities**, so infrequent features are never treated as an afterthought.

### Intensity guide

| Frequency | Intensity | Family examples |
|---|---|---|
| Daily / core (e.g. sending tokens) | **Subtle**: small details that never get in the way | Commas shift smoothly into place as digits are typed; an easter egg appears only when the amount exceeds the balance |
| Occasional | **Medium**: satisfying feedback that rewards the action | Tokens tumble into a skeuomorphic trash can, with a satisfying sound; drag-to-reorder with appealing stacking animations; chart scrubbing flips the up/down arrow along with the numbers; stealth mode shows a gentle shimmer to signal that hidden values are still updating |
| Rare / milestone (e.g. creating a wallet, finishing a backup) | **High**: mark the occasion | An interactive animation for wallet creation; confetti after a successful backup to reward an essential security task |
| First use | **Guiding**: friendly orientation | The in-app browser's empty state shows an animated arrow pointing to "new tab" |

### Surprise and easter eggs

Surprise is a strong lever. Easter eggs belong where a feature is used **just often enough** for the surprise to be enjoyable rather than annoying, and ideally they are **hidden in plain sight**.
- Example: tapping the QR code sets off a gentle ripple; swiping a finger across it turns the dots into something like sequins. The discovery is the delight.

## Rules

1. **Polish evenly first.** Do not add flourishes to a product with neglected corners.
2. **Rank features by frequency** and assign an intensity from the guide above.
3. **High-frequency actions:** use only details that do their job without becoming frustrating. Never slow down the core loop.
4. **Rare and important moments:** celebrate them. Don't let streamlining make a significant action feel mundane or hide what it means. Highlight its importance instead.
5. **Delight carries meaning.** The best touches also communicate something (the shimmer says "still updating", the confetti says "you're protected now", the arrow says "start here").
6. **Reward time and emotional investment.** The aim is to value the user, not just to entertain them. Entertainment is a welcome side effect and can turn an occasional visit into a habit.
7. **Sound is a tool.** A well-chosen sound effect on completing an action adds satisfaction. Use it sparingly.

## Finding delight opportunities (procedure)

1. List every feature and estimate how often it is used.
2. Mark the *significant but infrequent* moments: setup, backup, first use, milestones, completions.
3. For each one, ask what emotional beat it deserves (relief, pride, reassurance, curiosity) and design a moment that delivers that beat.
4. For frequent features, look for one micro-detail that makes them smoother, and stop there.
5. Look for one or two surfaces that are used a moderate amount and are good spots for a hidden easter egg.
6. Re-check that nothing added slows down a core task.
